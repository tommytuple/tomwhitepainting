Earthy Paint Colours: Clay, Ochre, Olive and Warm Stone

Earthy colours are the ones that look like something: clay, ochre, olive, dust, stone. They are pigments people have been putting on walls for several thousand years, which is roughly why they are so easy to live with.

What makes them work is the undertone — the yellow, the red or the green sitting underneath the colour you think you are looking at. Get two earthy colours with fighting undertones next to each other and the whole scheme goes slightly sour. Every colour below lists its undertone so you can keep them talking to each other.

Every colour below is a real paint, and the light reflectance values and undertones are measured rather than guessed. They are still screen versions of paint though, so order a sample pot and look at it on your own wall, in your own light, before you buy ten litres of anything.

Making earth tones work together

Pick one undertone and stay with it. A yellow-undertone stone next to a red-undertone clay will read as dirty rather than warm. The undertone is listed on every colour here; matching it is most of the job.

Earth tones want warm light. Under a 2700K bulb they glow; under a cool 4000K one they go grey and flat. If you have cool LEDs throughout, change those before you change the paint.

They are forgiving on imperfect walls, which makes them a genuinely good choice for an old house. The slight irregularity of lime plaster or a hundred-year-old wall reads as texture rather than as a defect.

Mid-LRV earth tones — the 25 to 50 range — are the most useful colours in decorating. Dark enough to have character, light enough to use across a whole room without the place closing in.

Common questions

What are earth tone paint colours?

Colours drawn from natural pigments — ochres, clays, umbers, olives and warm stones. In practical terms they are colours with a warm yellow, red or brown undertone and relatively low saturation, which is why they sit comfortably in a room rather than shouting.

Are earthy colours warm or cool?

Almost always warm, though olives and some stones sit close to neutral. Every colour on this page lists its measured warmth and its undertone, so you can tell the genuinely warm ones from the ones that will go grey on a north wall.

Which earthy colour works best in a north-facing room?

One with a yellow or red undertone and an LRV above about 35. India Yellow, Sudbury Yellow and Clay all push warmth back into a cold room. Avoid the grey-greens in a north-facing space unless you want the room to feel cool.

Do earth tones work in a modern house?

Yes, and arguably better than in a period one, because a modern room with flat plaster and large windows needs the warmth. The thing to avoid is pairing them with cool grey flooring, which fights the undertone.
