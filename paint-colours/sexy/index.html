Sexy Paint Colours: Deep, Dramatic Shades That Actually Work

Every list of “sexy” paint colours I have ever read is a mood board. Here is the decorator’s version: twelve colours I would actually put on a wall, with the numbers that tell you whether they will work in your room.

The number that matters is the light reflectance value, the LRV. It runs from 0 for black to 100 for white, and it tells you how much light the colour throws back into the room. Everything on this page sits under 15. That is the point of them — and it is also the thing people get caught out by.

Every colour below is a real paint, and the light reflectance values and undertones are measured rather than guessed. They are still screen versions of paint though, so order a sample pot and look at it on your own wall, in your own light, before you buy ten litres of anything.

Before you commit to a dark colour

A dark colour eats light. In a north-facing room with one small window it will read as flat and grey for most of the day. In a room with a decent window, or one you mainly use after dark, the same colour comes alive. Dark colours are for evening rooms: bedrooms, dining rooms, snugs, downstairs loos.

Finish does more work than the colour does. A dead flat matt swallows light and makes the colour look deeper and softer. An eggshell or a satin bounces light off the surface and makes the same colour look harder and glossier. If you want drama rather than severity, go matt.

Dark colours show every defect in the plaster, because the sheen difference over a filled patch is far more visible than it is under white. Budget for the preparation, not just the paint.

Two coats will almost never do it over a light wall. Deep reds and inky blues routinely want three, sometimes four, and a tinted primer underneath saves you a coat of expensive topcoat.

Common questions

Do dark paint colours make a room look smaller?

Not in the way people mean. A dark colour hides where the wall ends, so the corners of the room stop announcing themselves and the space reads as deeper, not smaller. What dark colours genuinely do is make a room darker, which is only a problem if you needed the light.

How many coats of a dark colour will I need?

Over an existing light wall, expect three. Deep reds and strong blues sometimes want four. A grey or tinted primer close to the final colour will usually save you a full topcoat, which pays for itself on designer paint.

What finish is best for a dark colour?

A flat or dead matt emulsion on walls, because it absorbs light and keeps the colour soft. Use eggshell on woodwork, where you need the durability. Avoid silk on a dark wall entirely — it will show every roller mark.

Which rooms suit dark paint colours?

Rooms you use in the evening, and rooms that already have decent natural light. Bedrooms, dining rooms, snugs and downstairs cloakrooms are the usual wins. A dark north-facing kitchen you use at eight in the morning is the usual regret.
