Brighton Paint Colours: Regency Stucco, Beach Hut Pastels and Sea Greens

I have been painting houses in Brighton and Hove for thirty years, and the city has a palette whether it means to or not. Regency cream on the stucco, washed-out pastels along the seafront, and a particular grey-green that turns up on every other front door between Kemptown and Hove.

These are real paints with their light reflectance values and undertones, chosen for how they behave in this specific light. The sea throws a great deal of bright, slightly cool light at the front of a building here, and colours that look right inland can look bleached out by eleven in the morning.

Every colour below is a real paint, and the light reflectance values and undertones are measured rather than guessed. They are still screen versions of paint though, so order a sample pot and look at it on your own wall, in your own light, before you buy ten litres of anything.

Painting a house by the sea

Salt air is the whole problem. Exterior paint on a seafront building has a materially shorter life than the same paint two miles inland, and the south and west elevations go first. Expect to repaint exposed elevations more often than the back of the house.

On Regency and Victorian stucco, use a breathable masonry paint. Those walls are solid, with no cavity and usually no damp proof course, and they rely on being able to dry outwards. A plastic film paint traps moisture behind it and the render blows off in sheets a few winters later.

Strong colours fade faster here. The light off the water is harsh and south-facing fronts take a beating — which is part of why the traditional seafront palette is pale. The pastels are not just a style choice, they weather better.

If you are in a conservation area — and a great deal of central Brighton and Hove is — check with the council before you change an exterior colour. Listed buildings have tighter rules again.

Common questions

What colour are Brighton's Regency houses painted?

The stucco fronts of the Regency terraces in Brighton and Hove are traditionally a warm off-white or pale cream, meant to imitate Bath stone. Farrow & Ball New White and Little Greene Slaked Lime are both close to the traditional colour in a modern breathable masonry paint.

What paint should I use on a house by the sea?

A breathable masonry paint on solid walls, and a high quality exterior eggshell on joinery. Salt air and driving rain shorten the life of exterior coatings noticeably on the seafront, and a non-breathable paint on old solid render will trap moisture and blow the surface off.

Can I paint my house any colour in Brighton?

Not everywhere. Much of central Brighton and Hove sits in a conservation area, and many seafront terraces are listed, which restricts exterior colour changes. Check with Brighton & Hove City Council before changing an exterior colour on a period building.

How often does a seafront house need repainting?

More often than an inland one, and the exposed elevations go first. The south and west faces of a seafront building take the salt and the weather, and will usually want attention well before the sheltered sides do.
